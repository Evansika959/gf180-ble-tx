This block is the BLE modulator (ble_tx) and its UART receiver, flattened into one
netlist with seven ports and no busy pin: about 50,000 um2 of cells after
synthesis, of which the UART is about 4,500 um2. The baseline n0 is the answer of
the first search of this design with a safer slew margin: a die 270 um wide,
fanout limit 8, slew margin 26, clock wire length 120, buffer spacing 72, clock
buffers capped at clkbuf_2, 40 sinks per leaf buffer. It signed off with no
violation of any kind after five fix passes: about 63,000 um2 of cells, 1.59 mW,
setup slack about 100 ns, hold slack 0.22 ns. A slew margin of 22 left two
slow-corner slew violations that the fix passes could not clear, so treat margins
below 26 as risky and evaluate them. The first search got its gains from the clock
tree, around wire lengths of 115 to 125. A plain LibreLane run of this netlist
failed detailed placement at 260 um, and 270 um is where the density floor reaches
its cap, so the narrowest legal width is 270 um unless that cap is raised.

Die area carries the most weight, but read objective for the narrowest width
allowed before branching FP: if it is still 270 um, the gains left are in cell area
and power, from placement (density, slew margin, wire length weight, fanout) and the
clock tree.

Nothing may be violated at signoff: no slew or capacitance violation at any corner
the gate reads, and no fanout violation either. The fanout limit is checked against
clock-tree buffers too, and those drive up to 8 sinks with the present clock tree:
with a limit of 6 the clock buffers were reported over it, with 8 they were not. So
keep MAX_FANOUT_CONSTRAINT at 8 or above unless a CTS change makes the clock
buffers drive fewer sinks, and check any such result with an evaluation.

Power is measured from a simulated advertising packet loaded through the UART pin
on each result's own netlist, so a small change in it is real; it carries a small
weight. Most of it is the clock network, as in ble_tx.
